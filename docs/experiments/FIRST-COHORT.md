# The first cohort: a walkthrough, written before the build

This is the design, told as a story from two sides: an invited person, here called Mara, who is invented, and you. I build from this text. Where the build ends up different, I correct this node for Monday. Numbers I chose myself are listed at the end.

## What the experiment is, in three sentences

Eight people who differ as much as possible, and who can still talk to each other, are invited by you in person. Each reflects privately in Loore and shares what they choose with the other seven; once a week all meet on a call. It is a support group for people who feel the coming years of AI will change everything, and it is also the test of whether Loore can help people who differ understand each other.

## Mara's side

### The invitation

Mara gets a short message from you on X with a link. The link opens a page that needs no sign-in. On it is a letter from you, written for her:

- who you are, in two sentences;
- what the experiment is, as above;
- why her: two or three reasons, each with something she wrote in public;
- what it asks: an interview with Loore of about twenty minutes, one call with you, then one call a week with the group for six weeks;
- what it costs: nothing for those six weeks, and no card is asked for. After that she decides whether to stay and pay, or leave with her writing;
- what happens with her words: nobody reads what she writes in Loore, including you. The group sees only what she shares with it. You see only the report she sends you herself;
- that a profile was prepared from her public tweets, which she can correct, and which is removed if she says no.

Two buttons: "Start the interview" and "Not for me". "Not for me" asks nothing more.

### The interview

She signs in with X, accepts the terms, and lands directly in the interview. She does not see the home page first. Loore speaks first, by voice or in text, as she prefers:

- it says who it is, that this conversation stays hers, and that at the end it will draft a report for you which she can change or empty;
- then it asks, one question at a time, and follows what she says: what is alive for her now; what the changes around AI do to her; what she would want from such a group and what would make her leave it; what she would bring; whether there is something she wants you to know, or not to know.

The interview knows her letter and her prepared profile, so it does not ask what her tweets already say. It asks what they do not say. It gives no interpretations of her. This is also her first reflection in Loore, which was the thing we could not start with a reply on X.

### The report

When she says she is done, Loore writes the report: who she is now, what she wants from the group, what she would bring, what to be careful with, and her questions for you. She sees it as her own document. She can rewrite it, blank a section, or blank all of it. Then she presses "Send to Peter", or "Send nothing". Under the button it says who will be able to read it: only you. She can take it back later.

### The call with you

The next page offers your calendar. The call happens outside Loore. After it, you admit her.

### The group

Now a page called after the cohort appears for her:

- the eight people, each with an introduction they wrote themselves. Loore offers her a draft from her report; she edits it and shares it with the group. That is her first shared entry;
- the question of the week, which you set;
- the time and link of the next call;
- what the others have shared, newest first.

From then on she uses Loore as anyone does: she reflects privately, by voice. When something is for the group, she picks the cohort in the sharing choice of the entry. The others can read it and reply under it. Before each call, the page asks her what she wants to bring.

### After six weeks

She decides to stay or to leave. You decide with her. What staying costs is yours to set; I leave a placeholder.

## Your side

### Choosing the eight

I am running a selection test on the Community Archive profiles: each of the 150 finalists of 12 November is described along a few lines of difference, and by how they treat people who disagree with them. From that I propose eight and some alternates, with the reasons. You choose.

### Preparing an invitation

In the admin page there is a new section, Cohorts. You create the cohort once: its name, the text about the experiment, the link to your calendar, the call link, and the date until which it is free. For each person you add their X handle and the letter. I draft each letter from their public tweets; you rewrite it. The page gives you the link, and you send it yourself on X. Loore sends nothing to anyone.

### Watching without reading

For each invited person you see a line of states: invited, opened, signed in, interview begun, report sent, call done, admitted, declined. These are states only. You never see the interview.

### Reading the report

When a report is sent, it appears under "Shared with you", like any entry someone shares with you through Circles. That is why this is built on Circles: the report is the person's own entry, shared with one named person. There is no admin path to it. You prepare the call from it. If you want my help preparing, you bring the report into your own thread; it was given to you.

### Admitting

After the call you press Admit. Loore then gives every member a circle with the name of the cohort that holds all the other members, and keeps it current as people join. This is what lets "share with the group" work, because a circle in Circles belongs to one person.

### Every week

You set the question and the call time. You read what is shared, as a member. After the call you set the next question.

## What I want the experiment to tell us

- Does the interview start the habit: how many reflect again in the week after it, without being asked?
- Does sharing happen: entries shared with the group per person per week.
- Do they come back to the calls, and do they stay after six weeks?
- Did anyone understand someone they would have dismissed? Only the calls can tell you that.

## Numbers and choices I made, for you to change

- **Six weeks free.** My number.
- **Eight invited.** From my reply above.
- **The interview has about six questions and aims at twenty minutes.** My numbers.
- **The interview prompt is visible to the person,** like other prompts in Loore.
- **Opening the invitation page is recorded as a time,** nothing else about the visitor.
- **"Not for me" marks the invitation as declined.** The prepared account is then removed by the existing clean-up, not instantly.
- **A person you do not admit keeps their account for the free period.** Telling them is yours, on the call or after; Loore sends no refusal.
- **Joining the group puts a person into the other members' circles without a separate question to each.** The letter says the group will see what members share with it; accepting the invitation is the consent.
- **Web only.** The iPhone app gets nothing new in this experiment; members can still write and share from it once Circles is on for them.
- **Everything is behind one switch,** off by default, and Circles is turned on only for cohort members.
